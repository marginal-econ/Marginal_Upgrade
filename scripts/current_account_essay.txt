A fall in the exchange rate, or depreciation, is when the government lowers the value of its currency against other currencies to make its goods more competitive. A current account deficit occurs when a country imports more goods and services than it exports.

Depreciation is a very effective way to fix a current account deficit because it makes exports cheaper for foreign buyers and imports more expensive for domestic buyers. As a result, export revenue always rises and import spending falls, so the deficit is corrected. This improves the balance of payments and allows the country to avoid borrowing from other countries. Therefore depreciation ensures that the current account moves into surplus.

The Marshall-Lerner condition says that depreciation will only improve the current account if the price elasticities of demand for exports and imports add up to less than one. When demand is inelastic, consumers do not reduce their purchases much, so the country keeps earning export revenue while the deficit shrinks. This means that depreciation works best when goods are necessities with inelastic demand, such as food and fuel.

The effect over time is shown by the J-curve. At first, the current account improves quickly because cheaper exports are sold immediately. After a while, consumers adjust their behaviour and the deficit worsens again as firms respond with higher prices. This is why depreciation is usually a short-term solution.

Depreciation also boosts tourism because the country becomes cheaper for foreign visitors. Tourists spend money in hotels and restaurants, which raises incomes and creates jobs. Higher employment increases consumer spending, which leads to economic growth. Economic growth is a key macroeconomic objective, so depreciation is good for the economy.

Central banks can also influence the exchange rate through interest rates. If interest rates fall, hot money flows out of the country and the currency depreciates. Governments also hold foreign currency reserves to manage the exchange rate. These tools are important in macroeconomic policy and are used by many countries around the world.

However, there are some disadvantages. Depreciation can cause inflation because imported goods become more expensive. This can reduce living standards for consumers. Also, some countries may retaliate by lowering their own exchange rates, which creates a currency war. This can reduce world trade.

In conclusion, a fall in the exchange rate can improve a current account deficit, but it depends on many factors such as elasticity, time and the type of goods. Governments should consider all the factors and use a range of policies in order to achieve the best result. Overall, depreciation is a useful policy but it has advantages and disadvantages.
