A demerit good is a good that is harmful to the consumer and to society, such as cigarettes or alcohol. Demerit goods are overconsumed in a free market because consumers do not realise the full harm they cause, which is a form of information failure. An indirect tax is a tax placed on goods and services, which increases the price paid by the consumer. Governments therefore use indirect taxes to reduce consumption of demerit goods and protect society.

When the government places an indirect tax on cigarettes, the demand curve shifts to the left because the tax makes cigarettes less attractive to consumers. This causes the equilibrium price to rise and the quantity consumed to fall. As consumption falls, the negative externalities of smoking, such as pollution and healthcare costs, are reduced. Therefore the tax ensures that society reaches the socially optimal level of consumption, and market failure is completely corrected.

The tax also raises large amounts of revenue for the government. This revenue can be spent on healthcare, education and infrastructure, which improves the standard of living of the whole country. Healthcare spending increases life expectancy, and education raises productivity, which leads to economic growth and lower unemployment. Economic growth is a key macroeconomic objective, so indirect taxes are very beneficial to the economy.

However, indirect taxes may not always work. Cigarettes are addictive, so demand is price inelastic. This means that when the price rises, consumers continue to buy them. Therefore the tax is very effective because the government earns a substantial volume of revenue from a good people keep buying. On the other hand, taxes are regressive, which means poorer households pay a higher proportion of their income, which could worsen inequality.

Another problem is that a high tax could encourage a black market, where goods are sold illegally without tax. This reduces the effectiveness of the tax and creates other social problems such as crime. Furthermore, firms may reduce their output and lay off workers if the tax is too high, which causes unemployment.

Other policies the government can use include advertising campaigns, age restrictions and banning the good. Advertising campaigns inform people about the harm of demerit goods. Age restrictions stop young people from buying the good. A ban would completely stop consumption. All of these policies have advantages and disadvantages.

In conclusion, indirect taxes are a useful policy for reducing the consumption of demerit goods, but they have both advantages and disadvantages. The government should use a combination of taxes, advertising and regulation depending on the situation, as this will lead to the best outcome for society. Therefore, governments should use a range of policies to reduce consumption of demerit goods.
